01 / The question

What is the human’s role and place?

If a bricklayer and a collaborative robot are constructing a wall together, what is the role and place of the human in that collaboration?

Australia’s housing crisis raises questions of labour, productivity, skill, safety and technology adoption. Construction work is situated, embodied, material and spatially contingent. This workshop asks how HCI can help design human-robot collaboration (HRC) in construction without treating the human as a residual operator or the robot as a simple replacement for labour.

We invite you to design through becoming: the bricklayer, the robot, the wall, the safety boundary and the spatial constraint. Using bricklaying tasks as a shared site for design inquiry, we will explore what humans do, notice, repair, coordinate and decide in collaborative construction work.

MORNING / BODYSTORMING

Embodied investigation.

Use body activation, role enactment and low-fidelity props to investigate three HRC bricklaying task scenarios. Develop human role maps describing what humans do, sense, decide, coordinate and repair.

AFTERNOON / SPATIAL PROTOTYPING

Immersive spatial prototyping.

Move from 2D layout planning into XR-based immersive prototyping to explore the spatial place of the human in HRC, including visibility, reach, safety zones, handovers, material flow and points of intervention.

02 / The day

From embodied exploration to design.

We will develop human role maps, spatial layout concepts and design recommendations for human-centred construction robotics.

03 / Take part

Different perspectives.
A shared workspace.

We welcome researchers, designers and practitioners interested in how people and robots work together.

Bring your perspective from HCI, architecture, construction, robotics, human factors, immersive technology, social science or design education. We plan to bring together approximately 12–20 participants in interdisciplinary groups.

No prior bricklaying experience is needed. You will be guided through the activities.

Participate in a way that works for you.

Movement activities can be adapted for seated or standing participation. Observing, sketching and directing are equally welcome ways to contribute. Headset use is optional; participants can also annotate layouts and join the discussion from outside the immersive scene.

Activities will not require strenuous movement or heavy lifting. To discuss accessibility requirements, contact Jasper.
