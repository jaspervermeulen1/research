About

I am a final-year PhD candidate at Queensland University of Technology and the Australian Cobotics Centre. My research examines how people shape human-robot collaboration, with a particular focus on multi-actor work in robot-assisted orthopaedic surgery.

Through video analysis, ethnographic observation, and interviews with surgical team members, I study how robotic surgery is made workable through distributed human coordination. My work shows that collaboration with robots is rarely a simple dyad between one human and one robot. In practice, it often depends on surgeons, nurses, assistants, robotic specialists, instruments, displays, spatial constraints, and organisational routines working together.

My doctoral research develops a socio-technical account of human-robot collaboration in the wild, contributing to HRI, HCI, CSCW, human factors, and robotics research.

Selected publications

My publications examine human-robot collaboration as it unfolds in real-world practice, with a focus on robot-assisted surgery, multi-actor teamwork, and the often invisible human work required to make robotic systems function safely and effectively.

ACM CHI 2026

The Choreography of Care: An Ethnographic Study of Human-Robot Collaboration in Makoplasty Surgeries

Jasper T. Vermeulen, James L. Dwyer, Alan G. Burden, Glenda Amayo Caldwell, Müge Belek Fialho Teixeira, Matthias Guertler, Ross Crawford

An ethnographic study of robot-assisted orthopaedic surgery showing how collaboration with a surgical robot is coordinated across surgeons, nurses, assistants, robotic specialists, instruments, displays, and the operating theatre itself.

Jasper T. Vermeulen, Müge Belek Fialho Teixeira, Glenda Amayo Caldwell, Alan G. Burden, Matthias Guertler

Human-centred strategies for improving efficiency in Makoplasty surgeries

Jasper T. Vermeulen, Glenda Caldwell, Müge Belek Fialho Teixeira, Alan Burden, James Dwyer, Matthias Guertler

Research themes

HRC in robot-assisted surgery

Ethnographic research examining how surgeons, nurses, assistants, and robotic specialists coordinate robotic orthopaedic surgery in real operating theatres.

Multi-actor HRC

Investigating how multiple humans collaborate around robotic systems through role typologies, coordination structures, and distributed teamwork models.

Theory and methodology

Advancing qualitative and socio-technical methods for HRI research, including STGT applications for analysis and theory generation.

HRC in construction robotics

Developing acceptance frameworks for construction human-robot collaboration, including adoption barriers and enablers in AEC environments.
